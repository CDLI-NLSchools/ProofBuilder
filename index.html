Proof Builder

Math 2201, Unit 7: Inductive and Deductive Reasoning (Indicators 2.5, 2.6)

This is practice: build proofs step by step, run number tricks, and design your own. Nothing is recorded and nothing leaves this page. One idea runs through everything here: examples are evidence, never a proof. A proof must cover every case, not just the cases you tried.

Guided Proofs

Each round states a claim about every case, not just one example. Work through three stages: choose a representation that forces the claim to be about all cases, assemble the proof steps in order, then choose the conclusion that the steps actually support.

    Number Trick Proofs

    A number trick always lands on the same result, no matter what number you start with. Run it on your own numbers first to see the constancy, then prove it with symbols. Remember: two starting numbers, or two hundred starting numbers, still would not prove it. The symbols do the proving, because they stand for every possible starting number.

        Create Your Own Trick

        Design your own number trick, starting from a number you call x. Add operations one at a time. The builder view shows your current expression in symbols, so you can see whether x is still in it. A trick that always gives the same answer needs x to disappear completely: the final expression must be a plain number.
